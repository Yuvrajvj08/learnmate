Can you explain the intuitive physical difference between Green’s Theorem and Stokes’ Theorem?
AR
psychology
LearnMate Socratic Engine POST /api/v1/tutor/ask [200 OK]

At their core, both theorems relate a macroscopic circulation around a closed boundary to the microscopic curling flux over the enclosed interior.

The physical departure lies entirely in dimension and manifold orientation:

Green’s Theorem (Planar 2D) R² Flat Region
∮_C (P dx + Q dy) = ∬_D (∂Q/∂x - ∂P/∂y) dA

Measures microscopic eddy rotation strictly constrained to the flat coordinate plane. The rotational axis is permanently normal to the page (k̂).

Stokes’ Theorem (Spatial 3D) R³ Curved Surface
∮_C F • dr = ∬_S (∇ × F) • n̂ dS

Lifts the membrane into three-dimensional space like a wind-blown butterfly net or soap film. The normal vector n̂ continuously adapts along the curved topology.

Section 16.7: Stokes' Theorem Retrieved Chunk #16-7B
Theorem 16.7.1 (Stokes’ Theorem): Let S be an oriented, smooth surface bounded by a simple, closed, and piecewise-smooth boundary curve C having positive orientation. If F is a vector field whose components have continuous partial derivatives on an open region containing S, then:
∮_C F • dr = ∬_S (curl F) • dS
Notice that the boundary curve C inherits its traversal direction via the right-hand orientation thumb aligned to n̂.
