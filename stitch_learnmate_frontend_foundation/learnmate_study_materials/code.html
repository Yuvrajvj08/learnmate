Section 16.2: Line Integrals in Vector Fields. Let \(\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = P\mathbf{i} + Q\mathbf{j} + R\mathbf{k}\) be a continuous vector field defined on a smooth curve \(C\) parameterized by \(\mathbf{r}(t)\) for \(a \le t \le b\). The line integral of \(\mathbf{F}\) along \(C\) evaluates the net work done along the trajectory: \[\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_a^b \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) \, dt\]

Chunk #2 • Stokes' Theorem Vector Identity
Chunk ID: vec-chunk-02

Theorem 16.8 (Stokes' Theorem): Let \(S\) be an oriented piecewise-smooth surface bounded by a simple, closed, piecewise-smooth curve \(C\) having positive orientation. If \(\mathbf{F}\) has continuous partial derivatives on an open region that contains \(S\), then: \[\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S \text{curl}\,\mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}\]

Chunk #3 • Flux Integrals Across Closed Boundaries
Chunk ID: vec-chunk-03

Surface integrals of vector fields calculate the volume flux per unit time through a designated boundary. In physical modeling of fluid dynamics and electrostatics, divergence theorem (Gauss' Theorem) establishes the equivalence between the triple integral of divergence over a solid region \(E\) and the surface flux over boundary \(\partial E\).
